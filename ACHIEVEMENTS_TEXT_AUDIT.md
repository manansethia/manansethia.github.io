# Achievements Page — Text Audit

Status: implemented in the cloned repository on 7 September 2026. The edits were limited to the issues below; the page structure and longer personal stories were retained.

## Implemented pass

- Removed cash amounts and itemized personal prize lists. Project or school grants remain only as purpose-focused support without values.
- Softened promotional wording such as “prestigious,” “renowned,” “massive,” “flagship,” and “one of the world’s most.”
- Replaced repeated self-promotional phrases with the concrete action or result while keeping the birthday, lucky-loser/Yoda, and Rafflesia stories.
- Standardized `CodeWars`, `INSPIRE MANAK`, and singular `runner-up` placement wording; fixed the duplicated issuer and listed grammar errors.
- Removed the unsupported TCS CodeVita claim from page metadata because no matching local achievement entry or evidence was present.
- Removed the conflicting HPE participant totals rather than publishing an unverified number; AIR 10 remains.

Two factual checks remain intentionally unresolved: whether `highest-rated Earth System Project` appears in the result documentation, and whether the Quick Navigation badge should count 41 top-level entries or 44 outcomes including bundled sub-results.

## Overall finding

The results are strong enough without promotional language. The page feels least professional where it repeatedly explains that an event was prestigious, renowned, massive, national-level, or difficult before reaching Manan’s actual result. The best future edit is not to remove personality; it is to make each entry arrive at the personal story or contribution faster.

A useful pattern for the later rewrite is:

> Result and date → one sentence of context → one sentence about contribution, learning, or a genuinely memorable moment.

## Keep the human parts

These details give the page a voice and should survive, with light copy-editing:

- the Ultimate Sports Quiz lucky-loser/Yoda story;
- the episode released on Manan’s birthday;
- the childhood Rafflesia buzzer memory;
- moments where Manan entered a college event as a school student, when relevant to the story rather than repeated as a boast;
- team members and collaborators where they materially shared the result.

## Remove or soften later

- Repeated organizer histories and claims such as “prestigious,” “renowned,” “massive,” “flagship,” and “one of the world’s most…” unless the distinction is necessary and sourced.
- Phrases such as “represented our school fiercely,” “proudly led,” “hard work paid off,” and repeated “only school student” claims. Replace them with the concrete action or result.
- Comparisons that diminish other participants, such as emphasizing that most finalists needed fifteen minutes after stating Manan’s own four-minute completion.
- Prize lists and cash values. They distract from the achievement and create the tone concern previously described as “glazing” or bad-omen detail.
- Repeated background paragraphs for recurring events such as Wild Wisdom; explain the event once at most.
